Counterevidence Liveness

How can evidence that should change a decision remain available and influential as it passes through a network of independently operated AI agents?

Our work studies a failure mode in multi-agent systems: the agents may collectively hold enough evidence to revise or stop an action, yet still proceed because a warning is withheld, dropped from a summary, routed elsewhere, never combined with complementary evidence, or arrives after the decision has become resistant to correction.

We use counterevidence liveness to describe whether such evidence is noticed, enters the workflow, survives communication and delegation, meets the evidence that completes it, and can still change the eventual action.

Recent work

Incentive Framing Shapes Costly Evidence Revelation in Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation

Manuscript under review · Manuscript PDF

We study when agents reveal privately held, decision-changing evidence. Across 60 high-stakes scenarios, four model families, and ten interaction rules, the disclosure rule substantially changes whether decisive evidence reaches the group. The experiments also show a second failure: evidence can be surfaced and still arrive too late to overturn an early collective interpretation.

Price, Order, Activate: Mechanisms for Costly Verifiable Evidence in Collective Prediction

Manuscript under review · Manuscript PDF

This work develops a theory of costly evidence revelation. Different evidence structures fail for different reasons: diffuse credit can suppress useful disclosures, speaking order can crowd out cheaper substitutes, and complementary evidence can deadlock because no first disclosure has enough value on its own. We study mechanisms based on marginal contribution, ordering, and activation to address these failures.

Current research direction

We are extending these ideas to longer, tool-using, multi-principal agent networks. The goal is to understand where counterevidence disappears, how communication topology and timing affect its survival, when a decision becomes resistant to correction, and whether lightweight decentralized mechanisms can keep unresolved warnings alive without requiring a single actor to see or control the full system.
