ICML 2026 Behavioral explanation

Directly Optimizing Natural Language Explanations for Behavioral Faithfulness: Simulatability and Recoverability

Advaith Malladi and Shashank Srivastava

OPeX trains an explainer according to how well its explanations let another learner predict a model’s decisions on seen and unseen inputs. It improves behavioral prediction across structured and text classification tasks, with a human study showing that the explanations also help people anticipate model behavior.

ACL 2026 Oral Reasoning and faithfulness

Is Chain-of-Thought Really Not Explainability? Chain-of-Thought Can Be Faithful without Hint Verbalization

Kerem Zaman and Shashank Srivastava

A model’s failure to mention an injected hint does not, by itself, establish that its reasoning is unfaithful. Through causal mediation analysis and comparisons of faithfulness metrics, we show that a hint can influence an answer through the chain of thought even when it is never explicitly acknowledged.

2026 Preprint Deceptive commitment

The Point of No Return: Counterfactual Localization of Deceptive Commitment in Language-Model Reasoning

Scott Merrill and Shashank Srivastava

By resampling continuations from successive reasoning prefixes, we locate points where a deceptive outcome becomes substantially more likely. Across five controlled environments and four reasoning models, we study how internal signals predict these transitions and how interventions on small sets of attention heads affect deceptive commitment across settings.

ACL 2022 Learning from language

CLUES: A Benchmark for Learning Classifiers using Natural Language Explanations

Rakesh R. Menon*, Sayan Ghosh*, and Shashank Srivastava

CLUES studies how classifiers can learn new concepts from explanations, using 36 real-world and 144 synthetic tasks. We introduce an entailment-based learning method and examine how the structure and linguistic content of an explanation affect generalization to new tasks.

Findings of EMNLP 2023 Language and behavior

Manipulating the Perceived Personality Traits of Language Models

Graham Caron and Shashank Srivastava

We investigate whether language models exhibit consistent perceived personality traits and whether textual context can predictably change them. Experiments with BERT and GPT-2 connect changes in questionnaire responses to traits expressed in generated text, alongside two datasets of human personality descriptions.
